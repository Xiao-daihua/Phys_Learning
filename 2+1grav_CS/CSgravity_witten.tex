\subsection{Geometric Phase Space Analysis}

\subsubsection{Discussion Set Up}

How to quantize a Theory, we usually start from analyzing the phase
space of the theory and then construct a poisson braket of the phase
space. Then one can transform the poisson bracket into commutator and
get the quantum theory.
Thus to study the quantum gravity in 2+1 dimensions, we need to first
learn about the phase space of the classical 2+1 dimensional gravity.

The phase space of a classical theory is often defined as:
\defi{
  Phase Space

  The phase space is the space of all $ q, \dot{q} $ at $ t = 0 $ surface.
}
However, this definition is not covariant and we need tons of
constraints to make it work, thus in 2+1 dimensional gravity, we use
the following definition:
\defi{
  Phase Space

  The phase space is the space of all classical solutions to the
  equations of motion Modulo gauge transformations.
}
For 2+1 dimensional gravity, this means that we want to find all the
solutions to:
\begin{align}
  R_{\mu\nu} = 0
\end{align}
To do this, we might first assume a spacetime topology of the
manifold we want to consider, we focus on a manifold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item With topology $ M \cong \mathbb{R} \times \Sigma $. where $ \Sigma
    $ is a genus $ g $ closed surface.
\end{itemize}

We now simplify the problem of analyzing the phase space of 2+1
dimensional gravity to finding the space of all solutions to the
vacuum Einstein equation $ R_{\mu\nu} = 0 $ on a manifold with
topology $ M \cong \mathbb{R} \times \Sigma $. These solutions can be
found by quotienting the Minkowski space by a discrete subgroup of
the isometry group of Minkowski space, which is the Poincare group $
ISO(2,1) $. In Witten's paper he gives out a analysis of those manifolds.

\subsubsection{General Flat Manifolds}

Now we try to construct more general flat manifolds with topology $ M
\cong [0,1] \times \Sigma_g $ by quotienting more general subspace of
the Minkowski space.

For a general flat 3-manifold $ \hat{M} $, we can construct its
simple connected universal cover $ M $. Due to the flatness and being
a universal covering space, $ M $ is isometric to the Minkowski space
or a subspace of the Minkowski space. And by definition, $ \hat{M} $
must be a quotient of $ M $, which is a subspace of the Minkowski
space, by a subgroup of the isometry group of the Minkowski space,
which is the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $.

Under this set up, we consider the fundamental group of $ \hat{M} $,
which is $ \pi_1(\hat{M}) $. Every element shall be a loop in $
\hat{M} $, which can be lifted to a path in $ M $. Then we can define
a element of the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $ $ \phi(\gamma) $ which
ensures that the
path $ [\gamma] $ in $ M $ which lifted from the loop $ [\gamma] $ in $
\hat{M} $, is mapped to a noncontractible loop after the quotienting
by $ \phi(\gamma) $.

Thus we can see that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every flat 3-manifold give rise to a homomorphism from $
    \pi_1(\hat{M}) $ to the Poincare group $
    ISO(2,1) $. It can be constructed by quotienting Minkowski space
    by the image of the homomorphism.
\end{itemize}

Thus, the phase space of 2+1 dimensional gravity can be identified
with the following space:
\begin{align}
  \text{Phase Space} = \text{Hom}(\pi_1(\Sigma_g), ISO(2,1))/\sim
\end{align}
Here $ \sim $ means that we quotient by the conjugation of the
Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $. We know that if two homomorphisms $
\phi_1, \phi_2 $ are related as:
\begin{align}
  \phi_1(\gamma) = g \phi_2(\gamma) g^{-1} \quad \forall \gamma \in ISO(2,1)
\end{align}
Then they give rise to the same manifold under quotient.

If we want to consider the topology of $ \hat{M} = \Sigma_g \times [0,1] $,
then the fundamental group of the manifold is $ \pi_1(\hat{M}) =
\pi_1(\Sigma_g) $. Thus, the phase space of 2+1 dimensional gravity
with topology $ M \cong [0,1] \times \Sigma_g $ is:
\thm{\label{thm:Geophasespace}
  Geometric Analized Phase Space of 2+1 Dimensional Gravity

  The phase space of 2+1 dimensional gravity with topology $ M \cong
  [0,1] \times \Sigma_g $ is:
  \begin{align}
    \text{Phase Space} = \text{Hom}(\pi_1(\Sigma_g), ISO(2,1))/\sim
  \end{align}
  where $ \sim $ means that we quotient by the conjugation of the
  Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $.
}

\textbf{Parameter Counting}

We then count the dimension of the phase space. For a surface $
\Sigma_g $, its fundamental group can be generated by $ 2g $
generators $ a_i, b_i $ with one relation:
\begin{align}
  \pi_1(\Sigma_g) = \langle a_1, b_1, \cdots, a_g, b_g |
  \prod_{i=1}^g a_i b_i a_i^{-1} b_i^{-1} = 1 \rangle
\end{align}
Thus a homomorphism from $ \pi_1(\Sigma_g) $ to the Poincare group
$ ISO(2,1) $ is determined by the image of the generators $ a_i, b_i $
in the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $. Which lead to $ 2g-1 $ parameters
with each parameter the dimension of the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $,
which is 6. Moreover, we need to quotient the phase space by the
conjugation of the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $, which lead to a
reduction of $ 6 $ parameters. Thus, the dimension of the phase space is:
\begin{align}
  \text{dim(Phase Space)} = (2g-1) \cdot 6 - 6 = 12g - 12
\end{align}

\subsection{EH Action of Vielbein Formalism}

The Vielbein formalism of gravity can be written from a action
principle. We can prove that:
\thm{
  Einstein-Hilbert Action in Vielbein Formalism

  The Einstein-Hilbert action in vielbein formalism in 4D can be written as:
  \begin{align}
    S_{EH} = \displaystyle\frac{1}{2} \int_{\mathcal{M}}
    \epsilon_{abcd} e^a \wedge e^b \wedge R^{cd}
  \end{align}
}
If we write this action in a normal integral (not differential form
integral), we can get:
\begin{align}
  S_{EH} = \displaystyle\frac{1}{2} \int_{\mathcal{M}} d^4x
  \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} \epsilon_{abcd} e_\mu{}^a e_\nu{}^b
  R_{\rho\sigma}{}^{cd}
\end{align}
We can prove that viration of this action with respect to the
vielbein $ e_\mu{}^a $ and the spin connection $ \omega_\mu{}^a{}_b $
gives us the Einstein equation and the torsion free condition respectively.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Viration of $ e_{\mu}{}^a $
\end{itemize}
We vary the action with respect to the vielbein $ e_\mu{}^a $ and we can get:
\begin{align}
  \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} \epsilon_{abcd} e_\nu{}^b
  R_{\rho\sigma}{}^{cd} = 0
\end{align}
Now we can exactly prove this is the Einstein field equation. Notice that:
\begin{align}\label{eq:epsilone}
  \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} e_{\mu}{}^a e_\nu{}^b e_\rho{}^c
  e_\sigma{}^d = \epsilon^{abcd} \det(e)
\end{align}
where $ det(e) $ can be calculated by the following formula:
\begin{align}
  det(e)^2 det(\eta) = det(e^T \eta e) = det(g)
\end{align}
Thus we can get that:
\begin{align}
  det(e) = \sqrt{-det(g)}
\end{align}
By pulging \cref{eq:epsilone} into the viration equation and using
the property of $ \epsilon $ symbol, we can get:
\begin{align}
  R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} R g_{\mu\nu} = 0
\end{align}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Viration of $ \omega_\mu{}^a{}_b $
\end{itemize}
The scheme is rather similar, we use the Cartan structure equation to
rewrite the action in terms of $ \omega^a{}_b $ explicitly and then
vary the action with respect to $ \omega_\mu{}^a{}_b $. The resulting
equation is just the torsion free condition:
\begin{align}
  de^a + \omega^a{}_b \wedge e^b = 0
\end{align}
or in component form:
\begin{align}\label{eq:torsionfree}
  D_\mu e_\nu{}^a - D_\nu e_\mu{}^a = 0
\end{align}
\rmk{
  Note that in fact we can not just assume this action to hold and
  say its equivalent to the Einstein gravity. Because in the
  deduction, we make an assumption that the vielbein is invertible,
  which is not generally guaranteed by the action itself (A general
  dynamical field are not assumed to have the component of a invertible matrix)

  However, in our following discussion we will not consider this
  problem. In modern topics, refer to
  \cite{eberhardtOffshellPartitionFunctions2022}, we may see that for
  AdS3 gravity, we can avoid the problem of $ e_i{}^a $ being
  non-invertible by considering the Teichmuller Component of the phase space.
}

\subsection{Chern-Simons Theory}

A Chern-Simons Theory is given by a gauge field $ A $ associated to a
gauge group $ G $ and on which we define a non-degenerate invariant
bilinear form $ Tr( *, *) $. The action of the Chern-Simons theory is given by:
\begin{align}
  S_{CS} = \displaystyle\frac{k}{4 pi} \int_{\mathcal{M}}
  \mathrm{Tr}\left( A \wedge dA + \frac{2}{3} A
  \wedge A \wedge A \right)
\end{align}
Here by $ Tr A \wedge A \wedge A $ we mean $ Tr(A_i, [A_j,
A_k]) dx^i \wedge dx^j \wedge dx^k  $ Thus, if we write the
differential form integral into
a normal integral, the action can be written as:
\begin{align}
  S_{CS} = \displaystyle\frac{k}{8 \pi} \int_{\mathcal{M}} d^3x
  \epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
  \mathrm{Tr}\left( A_\mu
    (\partial_\nu A_\rho - \partial_{\rho} A_\mu ) + \frac{2}{3} A_\mu
  [A_\nu, A_\rho] \right)
\end{align}
\rmk{
  Note that the additional factor of $ 1/2 $ in the second term is
  due to the convention of components of wedge product.
}

\subsection{3D Gravity as Chern-Simons Theory}

\subsubsection{Motivation}

Similar to the 4D case the EH action of 3D gravity in vielbein
formalism can be written as:
\begin{align}
  S_{EH} =  \int_{\mathcal{M}}
  \epsilon_{abc} e^a \wedge R^{bc}
\end{align}
if we write $ R^{ab} $ out using cartan structure equation, we see
this resembles the Chern-Simons action. Thus we are motivated to
rewrite the EH Action into a ISO(2,1) Chern-Simons action (To have
  the gauge group as the isometry group of Minkowski space, which is
the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $ is a natural choice). This rewriting
consist of 2 step to go:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The EH Action can be written as a Chern-Simons Action with
    ISO(2,1) gauge group and suitable Killing form.
  \item The gauge transformation of the Chern-Simons theory with
    ISO(2,1) gauge group is equivalent to the diffeomorphism and
    local lorentz transformation of vielbein and spin connection.
\end{enumerate}
\rmk{
  In fact, the second step is not achivable for the whole
  diffeomorphism group, but only for small diffeomorphisms. For large
  diffeomorphisms, we need extra gauge transformations to cover.
}

\subsubsection{Killing Form of ISO(2,1) }

The first step we're facing is to find a non-degenerate invariant
bilinear form of the Poincare group $ ISO(2,1) $. For a general
Poincare Group $ISO(d-1,1)$, in fact we don't have a non-degenerate
invariant bilinear form, for we need a casimir like object to make it
invariant and give out a number.

However, in the special case of $ISO(2,1)$, we can find one due to the
property that the lorentz generator in 2+1 dimensions is $ J^{ab} $ a
generator with two antisymmetric indices, in 3d its equivalent to a
generator with one index $ J^a $ by using the epsilon symbol. Thus
one can find a non-degenrate casimir:
\begin{align}
  W = \epsilon_{abc} P^a J^{bc}
\end{align}
And as a rersult we define: $ J^a = 1/2 \epsilon^{abc} J_{bc} $ and
the Poincare algebra in 2+1 dimensions can be written as:
\begin{align}
  [J_a, J_b] = \epsilon_{abc} J^c, \quad [J_a, P_b] = \epsilon_{abc} P^c, \quad
  [P_a, P_b] = 0
\end{align}
a non-degenerate invariant bilinear form:
\begin{align}
  \langle P_a, J_b \rangle = \delta_{ab} \quad \langle P_a, P_b
  \rangle = 0 \quad \langle J_a, J_b \rangle = 0
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Rewriting EH Action into Chern-Simons Action}

With these preparations, we can rewrite the EH action into a
Chern-Simons action with gauge group $ ISO(2,1) $ and the
non-degenerate invariant
bilinear form we just defined.

We first define a gauge field $ A $ as:
\begin{align}
  A_\mu = e_\mu{}^a P_a + \omega_\mu{}^a J_a
\end{align}
Here if we use the Christoffel symbol as the connection, the
resulting spin connection $ \omega_\mu{}^{ab} $ is antisymmetric in
the indices $ a, b $ and thus we can write it as $ \omega_\mu{}^a =
1/2 \epsilon^{abc} \omega_\mu{}_{bc}$
by using the epsilon symbol.

We note some useful facts about the gauge field $ A $:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gauge Transformation:} Consider a infinitesimal gauge
    transformation with the parameter:
    \begin{align}
      u = \rho^a P_a + \tau^a J_a
    \end{align}
    is given by:
    \begin{align}
      \delta_G A_\mu = -D_\mu^A u = - \partial_\mu u - [A_\mu, u]
    \end{align}
    Here $ \delta_G $ stands for gauge transformation, later on we
    will see there are other transformations, eg. diffeomorphism and
    transformation generated by constriant in quantization. $
    D_\mu^A$ is the covariant derivative in the adjoint representation.
    If we write the above gauge transformation in terms of the
    vielbein $ e_\mu{}^a $ and the spin connection $ \omega_\mu{}^a
    $, we can get:
    \begin{align}\label{eq:gaugetransform}
      & \delta_G e_\mu{}^a = -\partial_\mu \rho^a - \epsilon^{abc} e_{\mu b}
      \tau_c - \epsilon^{abc} \omega_{\mu b} \rho_c \\
      &\delta_G \omega_\mu{}^a = -\partial_\mu \tau^a - \epsilon^{abc}
      \omega_{\mu b}
      \tau_c
    \end{align}
  \item \textbf{Curvature Tensor} The curvature tensor is given by:
    \begin{align}
      F_{ij}&=\left[{D}_{i},{D}_{j}\right]\\
      &=P_{a}\Big{(}\,\partial_{i}e_{j}^{\,a}-\partial_{j}e_{i}^{\,a}+\epsilon^{abc}\big{(}\,\omega_{ib}e_{jc}+e_{ib}\omega_{jc}\big{)}\Big{)}
      \\
      &+J_{a}\Big{(}\,\partial_{i}\omega_{j}{}^{a}-\partial_{j}\omega_{i}{}^{a}+\epsilon^{abc}\omega_{ib}\omega_{jc}\,\Big{)}\,.
    \end{align}
\end{itemize}
\rmk{
  In fact one can directly see that the two terms in the curvature
  tensor are just the EoM by viration of the EH action with respect
  to the spin connection and the vielbein respectively. Thus, the
  curvature tensor is zero, which is a standard Chern-Simons EoM, is
  equivalent to the EoM of the EH action.
}
With these preparations, we can prove the Chern-Simons action with
the above gauge field is exactly the EH action of 3D gravity in
vielbein formalism.
\begin{align}
  S& = \int_{\mathcal{M}} \mathrm{Tr}\left( A \wedge dA + \frac{2}{3} A
  \wedge A \wedge A \right) \\
  & = \int_{\mathcal{M}}\epsilon_{abc} e^a \wedge R^{bc} \\
  & \propto \int_{\mathcal{M}} \epsilon^{ijk}\left( e_{ia} (\partial_j
    \omega_k{}^a - \partial_{k} \omega_j{}^a) + \epsilon_{abc} e_i{}^a
  \omega_{j}{}^b \omega_{k}{}^c \right)
\end{align}

\subsubsection{GCT and LLT as Gauge Transformation}

Now we focus on the diffeomorphism of vielbein and spin connection.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diffeomorphism:}
    Vielbein and spin connection can both be viewed as a 1-form thus the
    diffeomorphism is given
    by the Lie derivative. We consider a infinitesimal diffeomorphism
    generated by a vector fiel $ -v^\mu $
    \begin{align}
      \delta_D e^a & = \mathcal{L}_{- v} e^a = - v^\mu
      (\partial_{\mu} e_\nu{}^a - \partial_\nu e_\mu{}^a) dx^\nu -
      \partial_\nu (v^\mu e_\mu{}^a) dx^\nu \\
      \delta_D \omega^a & = \mathcal{L}_{- v} \omega^a = - v^\mu
      (\partial_{\mu} \omega_\nu{}^a - \partial_\nu \omega_\mu{}^a) dx^\nu -
      \partial_\nu (v^\mu \omega_\mu{}^a) dx^\nu
    \end{align}
  \item \textbf{Local Lorentz Transformation:} The local lorentz
    transformation generated by $ \tau^a $ as a parameter is given by:
    \begin{align}
      \delta_L e^a & = \epsilon^{abc} e_b \tau_c \\
      \delta_L \omega^a & = d\tau^a + \epsilon^{abc} \omega_b
      \tau_c
    \end{align}
\end{itemize}
\rmk{
  note that generally local lorentz transformation is
  generated by $ \omega_{ab} $ antisymmetric parameter.
  However, in 3D we can rewrite it as $ \omega_a = 1/2 \epsilon_{abc}
  \omega^{bc} $ by using the epsilon symbol, thus we can write
  the local lorentz transformation in a more compact form.

  It is important to note that the inverse transformation is:
  \begin{align}
    \omega^a = 1/2 \epsilon^{abc} \omega_{bc} \quad \text{but} \quad
    \omega_{ab} = -\epsilon_{abc} \omega^c
  \end{align}
  This is due to the fact that $ \epsilon^{abc} = -\epsilon_{abc} $.
}
These seems extremely distinct from the gauge transformation we
defined before \cref{eq:gaugetransform}. However, we can prove that
they are equivalent.

\bigskip
\textbf{$ \tau^a$ transformation as LLT}

First we notice that the gauge transformation generated by $ \tau^a $
is exactly a LLT.

\bigskip
\textbf{On shell $ \rho^a $ transformation as diffeomorphism}

Yet the gauge transformation generated by $ \rho^a
$ is not exactly a diffeomorphism. We explicitly write it out:
\begin{align}
  \delta_G e_\mu{}^a & = -\partial_\mu \rho^a - \epsilon^{abc}
  \omega_{\mu b} \rho_c \\
  \delta_G \omega_\mu{}^a & = 0
\end{align}
now we separately evaluate the difference between the gauge
transformation generated by $ \rho $ and diffeomorphism generated by
$ v^\mu $. We can see, if we take $ \rho^a = v^\mu e_\mu{}^a$, the
difference is given by:
\begin{align}
  \delta_G e_\mu{}^a - \delta_D e_\mu{}^a  =  - v^\nu (D_\nu
  e_\mu{}^a - D_\mu e_\nu{}^a)  + \epsilon^{abc}
  v^\nu \omega_{\nu b} e_{\mu c}
\end{align}
We notice that the first term is just the torsion tensor, and on
shell condition \cref{eq:torsionfree} tells us that this is zero. And
what we have left is a LLT generated by parameter: $ \tau^a = v^\mu
\omega_\mu{}^a $. Similarly we can prove that the diffeomorphism of
the spin connection is also a LLT generated by the same parameter.

\subsection{3D Grvaity as Chern-Simons Theory: Cosmological Constant Case}

Now we want to consider the theory with cosmological constant added.
An analogue with the above theory with zero cosmological constant, we
can also rewrite the EH action with cosmological constant into a
Chern-Simons action.

The EH action with cosmological constant can be written in the
vielbein formalism as:
\begin{align}
  S_{EH} =& \int_{\mathcal{M}} \epsilon_{abc} \left( e^a \wedge R^{bc} +
  \frac{\Lambda}{3} e^a \wedge e^b \wedge e^c \right) \\
  \propto & \int_{\mathcal{M}} \epsilon^{ijk} \left( e_{ia} (\partial_j
    \omega_k{}^a - \partial_{k} \omega_j{}^a) + \epsilon_{abc} e_i{}^a
    \omega_{j}{}^b \omega_{k}{}^c + \frac{\Lambda}{3} \epsilon_{abc}
  e_i{}^a e_j{}^b e_k{}^c \right)
\end{align}
\rmk{
  In fact this is not the standard way and $ \Lambda $ is not the
  usual cosmological constant. It is in fact $ -
  \Lambda_{\text{canonical}} $. Here in this discussion we will
  follow witten's construction and note that $ \Lambda < 0 $ is dS
  and $ \Lambda > 0 $ is AdS, which is opposite to the standard
  convention. In the following discussion we will also follow this convention.
}
But now the gauge group instead of being the
isometry group of Minkowski space, which is the Poincare group $
ISO(2,1) $, is the isometry group of the spacetime with cosmological
constant, which is $ SO(2,2) $ for negative cosmological constant AdS and
$ SO(3,1) $ for positive cosmological constant dS.

The generators of the algebras can be written in a similar way:
\begin{align}
  [J_a, J_b]  = \epsilon_{abc} J^c \quad
  [J_a, P_b]  = \epsilon_{abc} P^c \quad
  [P_a, P_b]  = \Lambda \epsilon_{abc} J^c
\end{align}
We can see that when the cosmological constant $ \Lambda $ is zero,
the algebra reduces to the Poincare algebra in 2+1 dimensions. The
killing form of the algebra can also be defined in a similar way:
\begin{align}
  \langle P_a, J_b \rangle  = \delta_{ab} \quad \langle P_a, P_b
  \rangle  = 0 \quad
  \langle J_a, J_b \rangle  = 0
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Rewriting EH Action into Chern-Simons Action}

By rewriting the gauge field $ A $ in a similar way:
\begin{align}
  A_\mu = e_\mu{}^a P_a + \omega_\mu{}^a J_a
\end{align}
\rmk{
  This rewriting is not that inspiring in modern context, the next
  subsection I'll show that for AdS and dS we can rewrite the EH
  action into two Chern-Simons theory with gauge group $
  SL(2,\mathbb{R}) $ and $ SU(2) $ respectively, which is more useful
  in modern context.
}
We also note some useful facts of the gauge field $ A $:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gauge Transformation:} It resembles the original one
    but with an extra term in the transformation of $ \omega_\mu{}^a $:
    \begin{align}\label{eq:gaugetransformcos}
      & \delta_G e_\mu{}^a = -\partial_\mu \rho^a - \epsilon^{abc} e_{\mu b}
      \tau_c - \epsilon^{abc} \omega_{\mu b} \rho_c \\
      &\delta_G \omega_\mu{}^a = -\partial_\mu \tau^a - \epsilon^{abc}
      \omega_{\mu b}
      \tau_c - \Lambda \epsilon^{abc} e_{\mu b} \rho_c
    \end{align}
  \item \textbf{Curvature Tensor} The curvature tensor is given by:
    \begin{align}\label{eq:curvaturetensorcos}
      F_{ij}&=\left[{D}_{i},{D}_{j}\right]\\
      &=P_{a}\Big{(}\,\partial_{i}e_{j}^{\,a}-\partial_{j}e_{i}^{\,a}+\epsilon^{abc}\big{(}\,\omega_{ib}e_{jc}+e_{ib}\omega_{jc}\big{)}\Big{)}
      \\
      &+J_{a}\Big{(}\,\partial_{i}\omega_{j}{}^{a}-\partial_{j}\omega_{i}{}^{a}+\epsilon^{abc}\omega_{ib}\omega_{jc}+\Lambda
      \epsilon^{abc} e_{ib} e_{jc} \,\Big{)}\,.
    \end{align}
\end{itemize}

We can also rewrite the EH action with cosmological constant into a
Chern-Simons action with gauge group $ SO(2,2) $ for $ \Lambda>0 $ AdS and
$ SO(3,1) $ for $ \Lambda<0 $ dS and the
non-degenerate invariant bilinear form we just defined.
\begin{align}
  S_{CS} & = \int_{\mathcal{M}} \mathrm{Tr}\left( A \wedge dA + \frac{2}{3} A
  \wedge A \wedge A \right) \\
  & \propto \int_{\mathcal{M}} \epsilon_{abc} \left( e^a \wedge R^{bc} +
  \frac{\Lambda}{3} e^a \wedge e^b \wedge e^c \right) \\
\end{align}

Similarly, we can see that the curvature tensor is zero, which is a
standard Chern-Simons EoM, is equivalent to the EoM of the EH action
with cosmological constant. The torsion free condition is unchanged
and the second EoM cahnge into the Einstein equation with cosmological constant.

Moreover, we can also prove that the gauge transformation of the
Chern-Simons theory will be equivalent to the diffeomorphism and
local lorentz transformation of the vielbein and spin connection,
which is similar to the zero cosmological constant case.

\subsection{3D Grvaity as Chern-Simons Theory: AdS Case}\label{sec:AdSCS}
\rmk{
  In this subsection, we use the canonical cosmological constant
  convention $ \Lambda $. Not the one used in above subsection.
}

In fact with the cosmological constant, we can redefine two gauge
fields can rewrite the EH action into two Chern-Simons action with
gauge group $ SL(2,\mathbb{R}) $ for AdS and $ SU(2) $ for dS, which
is more useful in modern context. To my corrent interest, I will
focus on the AdS case. Discussion in this section mainly follows
\cite{compereAdvancedLecturesGeneral2019a}.

Here we turn back to the modern canonical way of writing the EH
action, for AdS case $ \Lambda = -1/l^2 $, the EH action can be written as:
\begin{align}
  S_{EH} &= \displaystyle\frac{1}{16 \pi G} \int_{\mathcal{M}} d^3 x
  \sqrt{-g} (R - 2 \Lambda)  \\
  &= \displaystyle\frac{1}{16 \pi G} \int_{\mathcal{M}} \epsilon_{abc}
  \left( e^a \wedge R^{bc} + \frac{1}{3 l^2} e^a \wedge e^b \wedge e^c \right)
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Rewriting EH Action into Two Chern-Simons Action}

The gauge group $ SO(2,2) $ is locally isomorphic to $ SL(2,\mathbb{R})
\times SL(2,\mathbb{R}) $. Thus, we can rewrite the gauge field $ A $
into two gauge fields $ A^+ $ and $ A^- $ with gauge group $ SL(2,\mathbb{R}) $.

First the $ SL(2,\mathbb{R}) $ algebra can be written as 3 generators
$ J_a $ with the following commutation relation:
\begin{align}
  [J_a, J_b] = \epsilon_{abc} \eta^{cd} J^d = \epsilon_{ab}{}^c J_c
\end{align}
and with a killing form:
\begin{align}
  \langle J_a, J_b \rangle =  \eta_{ab}
\end{align}
Then we can define the two gauge fields $ A^+ $ and $ A^- $ as:
\begin{align}
  A^{+a} = \omega^a + \frac{1}{l} e^a \quad A^{-a}  = \omega^a -
  \frac{1}{l} e^a
\end{align}
where the cosmological constant $ \Lambda $ is related to the AdS
radius $ l $ by $\Lambda = -{1}/{l^2}$.
\YL{[One shall check the definition of $ \epsilon_{abc} $
and be careful with the signs. But this is not conceptually important.]}

With these preparations, we can rewrite the EH action with
cosmological constant into a
Chern-Simons action with gauge group $ SL(2,\mathbb{R}) $ and the
non-degenerate invariant bilinear form we just defined. We note:
\begin{align}
  S_{CS}[A] = \int_{\mathcal{M}} \mathrm{Tr}\left( A \wedge dA + \frac{2}{3} A
  \wedge A \wedge A \right)
\end{align}
Then we can prove (I'm not proving it here):
\begin{align}
  S_{CS} & = \displaystyle\frac{k}{4 \pi} (S_{CS}[A^+] - S_{CS}[A^-] )\\
  & = \displaystyle\frac{1}{16 \pi G} \int_{\mathcal{M}}
  \epsilon_{abc} \left( e^a \wedge R^{bc} + \frac{1}{3 l^2} e^a
  \wedge e^b \wedge e^c \right) \\
  & = \displaystyle\frac{1}{16 \pi G} \int_{\mathcal{M}} d^3 x
  \sqrt{-g} (R + 2 \Lambda) - \text{boundary term}
\end{align}
Here to fit in the canonical normalization of the EH action, we have
defined the level $ k $ as:
\begin{align}
  k = \displaystyle\frac{l}{4 G}
\end{align}
With a standard Chern-Simons derivation, we can see that the EoM
gives us the flatness condition of the gauge field $ A^+ $ and $ A^-
$:
\begin{align}
  F^+ = dA^+ + A^+ \wedge A^+ = 0 \quad F^- = dA^- + A^- \wedge A^- = 0
\end{align}

\subsubsection{GCT and LLT as Gauge Transformation}

Here we still can prove the gauge transformation of the two
Chern-Simons theory can give us a diffeomorphism and a local lorentz
transformation.

I won't copy the proof here. The idea of
\cite{compereAdvancedLecturesGeneral2019a} is to calculate the gauge
transformation of  $ e_i{}^a $ by the gauge transformation of $ A^+ $
and $ A^- $ with parameter $ \lambda^+ $ and $ \lambda^- $ and given
the relation between $ e_{i}{}^a $ and $ g_{\mu \nu} $ prove that its
equivalent to a diffeomorphism of the metric:
\begin{align}
  \delta_G g_{\mu \nu} = \nabla_\mu \xi_\nu - \nabla_\nu \xi_\mu =
  \delta_D g_{\mu \nu}
\end{align}
